Full lesson: A beautiful old Italian town

Level: B2+
Format: one-to-one
Main aim: place several adjectives before a noun in a natural order
Secondary aim: describe places, objects and experiences in greater detail
Recycled language: fascinating, inspiring, surprising, comforting, confusing, overwhelming, recharge, unwind, come to mind, visual aid, first-aid kit, by myself, on my own, find myself doing something, gaze at, do wonders for my mood

2. Notice the pattern in context

Read the description.

A surprising weekend

When Victoria found out about a small historical town nearby, she was initially sceptical about visiting it. The online reviews were positive, but the photos showed only a few narrow old streets and some small grey stone buildings.

In the end, she decided to go by herself. When she arrived, she was surprised by the town’s atmosphere. At its centre stood a beautiful large medieval church with tall arched wooden doors. Beside it was a charming little Italian café with several round black metal tables outside.

Victoria ordered coffee and found herself gazing at an unusual colourful glass lamp above her table. The café also had an interesting old travel poster on the wall and a large antique wooden bookcase filled with books.

She had planned to stay for only an hour, but the place was so relaxing that she decided to immerse herself in the atmosphere. She walked through the streets, visited a museum and bought a small handmade leather notebook for herself.

By the end of the day, she felt relaxed and inspired. The trip had been an unexpectedly comforting way to unwind and recharge.

3. Discover the adjective sequence

Match the adjective categories with the examples.

Category	Examples
Opinion	beautiful, lovely, fascinating, useful
Size	large, small, tiny, enormous
Physical quality	narrow, thick, soft, rough
Age	new, old, ancient, modern
Shape	round, square, rectangular, curved
Colour	black, grey, turquoise, pale
Origin	Italian, Ukrainian, French, Hungarian
Material	wooden, leather, glass, metal
Type or purpose	travel, teaching, safety, coffee
Noun	bag, table, poster, aid
Put the categories in order

Use the examples from the text to complete the sequence:

opinion → ________ → physical quality → ________ → shape → ________ → origin → ________ → type/purpose → noun

The usual order

opinion → size → physical quality → age → shape → colour → origin → material → type/purpose → noun

Example

a beautiful small old round black Italian leather travel bag

This is grammatically possible, but native speakers rarely use so many adjectives together. Usually, two or three well-chosen adjectives sound more natural:

a beautiful old Italian bag
a small black leather bag
a practical leather travel bag

4. The most useful short version

5. Opinion versus description

Opinion adjectives usually come before factual or descriptive adjectives.

a fascinating historical documentary
an inspiring young Ukrainian artist
a beautiful old wooden house
an annoying new digital system
a confusing modern teaching method

Compare:

a beautiful old building

beautiful expresses the speaker’s opinion.
old gives factual information about the building.

11. Don’t use every adjective

Long adjective strings are grammatically possible, but they often sound heavy.

Too crowded

We entered a beautiful large old rectangular grey Hungarian stone historical building.

More natural

We entered a beautiful old stone building in Hungary. It was large and rectangular, with grey walls.

Rewrite naturally

Break each heavy description into two sentences.

She bought a useful small new rectangular red plastic first-aid kit.
We visited a fascinating enormous ancient square grey Italian stone building.
He showed us an interesting large old colourful Ukrainian educational poster.
They stayed in a charming tiny old white French wooden holiday house.
The teacher created a helpful large modern rectangular digital grammar visual aid.
12. A special case: adjectives joined by and

When adjectives describe the same general quality independently, we can often join them with and.

a calm and comforting atmosphere
a long and exhausting journey
an interesting and inspiring lesson
a small and uncomfortable room

These adjectives are more equal. Their order is less fixed.

Compare:

a beautiful old wooden house
Different categories: no commas or and.
a beautiful, peaceful and welcoming house
Several descriptions or opinions of equal importance.
Decide: fixed sequence or equal descriptions?
a fascinating old Italian town
a fascinating and inspiring town
a small round wooden table
a cold, dark and frightening room
a useful new digital visual aid
a long and exhausting journey
a confusing modern teaching platform
a calm and comforting atmosphere

18. Spot the hidden meaning change

Adjective position can sometimes change emphasis or meaning.

Compare:

An old friend

Usually means a friend you have known for a long time.

An elderly friend

Means a friend who is advanced in age.

A certain person

A particular person whose identity is not stated.

A person who is certain

A person who is sure.

The present situation

The situation now.

The people present

The people who are there.

Explain the difference:

a responsible person / the person responsible
a concerned parent / the people concerned
a complete disaster / the task is complete
a late colleague / my late colleague

This is an extension point. The main lesson focus remains adjective order before nouns.
